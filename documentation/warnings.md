# Fit warnings

Every warning a fit raises is also stored on the fit: `m.warnings` in Python,
`m$warnings` in R. This page lists all of them.

## How a warning looks

Each warning has a tier, a title and a message. It prints as:

```
Tier: Title. Message
```

For example:

```
Severe: Fit failed. The fitting algorithm failed and returned no estimates. Try a simpler random-effects structure or rescale the predictors.
```

Python and R add their own label in front of it. Python prints the warning class
(`FitFailedWarning: Severe: Fit failed. …`). R prints
`Warning message:` on the line above.

The stored entry has four fields: `tier`, `kind`, `title` and `message`. In
Python it is a `dict`, and `m.warnings` is a list of them. In R, `m$warnings`
is a `data.frame` with one row per warning. Match on `kind` in scripts. The
title and message text can change between versions; `kind` does not.

## Tiers

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Severe | Do not use these results until this is fixed. |
| Caution | The results are usable; read them with this in mind. |
| Note | Something was adjusted; the results are not affected. |

## Warnings

Text in `<angle brackets>` is filled in from the fit. Where the ports differ,
the Python text comes first and the R text second, separated by ` / `.

| Kind | Tier | Title | Message | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `search_limit` | Severe | Search stopped at its step limit | The search for the variance estimates reached its step limit before it settled. The estimates shown are the best point found; they are often close, but this is not checked. Do not use them until the fit converges. Try a simpler random-effects structure or rescale the predictors. For a GLMM whose inner steps also ran out of iterations, this sentence comes second: Some of its inner steps ran out of iterations. | LMM and GLMM. The optimizer (BOBYQA over the variance parameters) used its evaluation budget without meeting its tolerance, and `converged` is false. Everything is reported at the best point found: coefficients, standard errors, variance components and dispersion; fitted values and random-effect modes are empty. That point is usually close to the optimum, but nothing checks how close. The inner-steps sentence is added when a GLMM's inner penalized IRLS (PIRLS) solve reached its cap of 200 iterations during the search. There is no retry or multi-start; refit with fewer random effects or rescaled predictors. Chosen over `fit_failed` when the end point has a finite deviance and every estimated coefficient is finite. An LMM whose search reports convergence with fewer than two finite deviance evaluations (a degenerate surface) is also reported as `search_limit`, since it is not treated as converged either. |
| `fit_failed` | Severe | Fit failed | The fitting algorithm failed and returned no estimates. Try a simpler random-effects structure or rescale the predictors. For a GLMM whose inner steps ran out of iterations, this sentence comes second: Some of its inner steps ran out of iterations. | LMM and GLMM. The optimizer stopped on a degenerate configuration (a failed step, a non-positive-definite system, or a non-finite deviance at the end point), and `converged` is false. Every estimate is NaN, and fitted values and random-effect modes are empty. The inner-steps sentence works as for `search_limit`. Also raised for a mixed model with no fixed effects whose end point has no finite deviance. |
| `glm_diverged` | Severe | Fit diverged | The fit did not converge. This usually means a predictor, or a combination of predictors, predicts the outcome perfectly (separation), so some fitted probabilities go to 0 or 1. The coefficients are from the last step; standard errors are not reported. Check the data for separation. | GLM without random effects (IRLS), including the inner fit of a negative binomial GLM. `converged` is false when one of four checks fires: a linear predictor goes above 30 in absolute value after the third iteration; more than half the rows end with a working weight below 1e-5; X'WX is not positive definite; or the fit reaches the IRLS iteration cap (`MAX_IRLS_ITERS = 50`, `src/glm.rs`) without converging. The first two are the usual signs of complete or quasi-complete separation. The coefficients are the last iterate; the standard errors and the deviance are NaN on purpose, because under separation they would be huge and meaningless. The message depends on the family: separation for binomial, all-zero categories for Poisson and negative binomial, and an unsettled fit for Gamma and inverse Gaussian, whose fits skip the linear-predictor check. |
| `glm_diverged` | Severe | Fit diverged | The fit did not converge. This usually means that some category of a predictor, or some combination of predictors, has only zero counts, so some fitted counts go to 0. The coefficients are from the last step; standard errors are not reported. Check for categories whose counts are all zero. | Same as above. |
| `glm_diverged` | Severe | Fit diverged | The fit did not settle on an answer: the fitting steps stopped before converging. The coefficients are from the last step; standard errors are not reported. Check predictors with extreme values; with a link other than log, the log link is usually more stable. | Same as above. |
| `design_unsolvable` | Severe | Predictors could not be separated | The predictors could not be separated numerically, so no estimates were computed. Check for predictors that are copies or near-copies of each other. | Gaussian model without random effects (OLS). The Cholesky factorization of X'X failed, and `converged` is false. Exactly redundant columns are already dropped as aliased before this step, so this means columns that are redundant up to rounding. Every estimate is NaN. |
| `constant_response` | Severe | Response does not vary | Every value of the response is `<value>`, so there is nothing to estimate. Check the response column. / Check the response column and the rows kept by subset= and na.action. | Any model. Raised in place of the model-based non-convergence warnings when `converged` is false and every row of the response has the same value (after lowering: a two-column binomial response is the proportion). A binomial response of all 0 or all 1 is refused before any iteration. It takes precedence over the model-based kinds, including `design_unsolvable`. A constant non-zero response can still converge for some families (a constant Poisson or Gamma response, for example), and then nothing is raised; this kind is raised only when the fit did not converge. |
| `too_few_rows` | Severe | Too few rows | The model has `<p>` coefficient(s) to estimate but only `<n>` row(s), so no estimates were computed. Use more rows or fewer predictors. | Raised when `converged` is false and the number of rows is at most the number of estimated coefficients (those not dropped as aliased). OLS and GLM refuse such a design before fitting; a mixed model fails on it. The counts are written out in words the ports build: "1 coefficient", "3 coefficients", "1 row", "2 rows". |
| `no_coefficients` | Severe | Nothing to estimate | The model has no coefficients and no random effects, so there is nothing to estimate. Add an intercept or a predictor. | A model without random effects whose formula leaves no fixed-effect column, either because the formula has none (the formula lowering already refuses `y ~ 0`) or because every column was dropped as aliased. OLS and GLM refuse it before fitting. |
| `pirls_exhausted` | Caution | Last fitting step did not finish | The final step that computes the reported results ran out of iterations. The estimates and their standard errors may be less accurate than usual. Try simplifying the random effects or rescaling the predictors. | GLMM with the Laplace approximation only. After the outer search converges, the fit runs the inner penalized IRLS (PIRLS) solve once more at the final variance parameters; that solve produces the reported coefficients, random-effect modes, weights, deviance and both kinds of standard errors. This warning means that final solve reached its cap of 200 iterations without meeting its tolerance, so every reported number carries some extra numerical error. Cap-outs on trial points the optimizer later rejected do not affect any reported number; they are kept in the fit's notes and not raised. When the outer search also failed, the cap-out is reported inside `search_limit` or `fit_failed` instead. Inner solves under adaptive quadrature (nagq above 1) are not checked. |
| `nb_shape_unsettled` | Caution | Shape search did not settle | The search for the negative binomial shape parameter stopped at its limit of `<rounds>` rounds before it settled. The coefficients and standard errors are computed at the last value it reached, which may not be the best one. | Negative binomial GLM without random effects. The fit alternates between the coefficients (IRLS at a fixed shape) and the shape θ (maximised with the fitted means held fixed), as `MASS::glm.nb` does, for at most 25 rounds, and stops early once θ changes by less than 1e-6 relative. This warning means all 25 rounds ran. The coefficients and standard errors are refit at the reported θ, so they belong to one model. Mixed negative binomial models search θ together with the variance parameters and report a budget stop as `search_limit` instead. |
| `singular` | Caution | Singular fit | The random effects are too complex for the data: a variance is estimated at or near zero, or a correlation at or near −1 or 1. Consider removing the affected random effect. When the fit knows which components are affected, this follows: Affected: `<term>` in `<group>`, … | Raised when either of two checks fires. (a) The optimizer fixed a diagonal element of a random-effect Cholesky factor at its lower bound (1e-4 on glmm's internal scale). On the first term of a block this puts that term's variance at zero. On a later term it can instead mean the term is perfectly correlated with an earlier one, so its printed standard deviation can look ordinary while its correlation is ±1. (b) After fitting, some random-effect standard deviation is at most 1e-3 times the largest one. The terms after "Affected" are the ones fixed by (a); when only (b) fires, no term is named. The fixed-effect estimates are still valid; the affected variances and correlations are not reliable. glmm has no uncorrelated-random-effects syntax (`\|\|`), so the fix is to drop the affected random slope or grouping factor and refit. Singularity is not assessed on a fit that did not converge: the kernel never sets `singular` there (the post-hoc check and the boundary flags are gated on `converged`), so `singular` reads false on a non-converged fit, and the severe warning raised there already says not to use the estimates anyway. The ports still gate this warning on `converged` themselves as a defensive check, in case a future kernel change ever sets `singular` on a non-converged fit. |
| `ill_conditioned` | Caution | Nearly collinear columns | `<column>` is almost a combination of other columns in the model, so its standard error is large. Its estimate is still correct, but imprecise. The other columns involved are not named. Consider dropping or combining predictors that carry the same information. | Raised for OLS, GLM and LMM fits, never for a GLMM, which does not compute this check. glmm factors the fixed-effect cross-product (X'X; X'WX for a GLM; X'V⁻¹X at the fitted variance parameters for an LMM) without pivoting, in the column order of the design, and for each column computes the share of its sum of squares that is left after the earlier columns are removed. The warning fires when the smallest share is below 1e-12, and names the column with that share. At this level an OLS or GLM coefficient has lost roughly three significant digits to rounding. The named column can be the intercept, one level of a factor, or an interaction term; its partners are not identified, and which member of the group is named depends on column order. The column stays in the model and its estimate is unbiased but imprecise. This differs from aliasing: an exactly redundant column is dropped and reported as NaN. The share does not change when a column is multiplied by a constant, so rescaling does not remove the warning; centering variables used in interactions or polynomial terms can. |
| `hessian_se_fallback` | Caution | Simpler standard errors used | The usual standard errors could not be computed, so a simpler method was used. Its standard errors tend to be too small, so p-values and confidence intervals may look more precise than they are. Standard errors for the random-effect standard deviations are not available. | GLMM only, under the default `wald_se="hessian"` / `wald.se = "hessian"`. The default standard errors come from the joint Hessian of the Laplace deviance in the variance parameters and the fixed effects, computed exactly where the model shape allows it and by finite differences otherwise; this matches lme4's `glmer` default. The fallback fires when that Hessian is not positive definite, or when a finite-difference step gives a non-finite deviance. The standard errors reported instead come from the fixed-effect block alone (RX), which treats the variance parameters as known; this is what MixedModels.jl reports and what lme4 gives with `use.hessian = FALSE`, and for a GLMM it is anticonservative. The standard errors of the random-effect standard deviations need the joint Hessian, so they are NaN. Refitting with `wald_se="rx"` / `wald.se = "rx"` gives the same numbers without the failed attempt. |
| `agq_fallback` | Caution | Adaptive quadrature not used | nagq=`<k>` / nAGQ=`<k>` works only for binomial, Poisson, negative-binomial or Gamma models whose random effects are in one grouping factor, with at most 3 random effects in it. This model was fitted without adaptive quadrature. | Adaptive Gauss-Hermite quadrature is available for binomial, Poisson, negative-binomial and Gamma GLMMs, with any link, that have one grouping factor and at most 3 random effects in it (the intercept plus slopes). The integral over each group uses k^q quadrature points, so the cost grows fast with q; q ≤ 3 is the range glmm has validated. Outside that range a mixed model is fitted with the Laplace approximation, and the fit records nagq = 1. A Gaussian model, or a model without random effects, has nothing to approximate; nagq there is reported as `argument_ignored`, not as this warning. The Laplace approximation can understate random-effect variances for binary or low-count data with few observations per group. |
| any other kind | Caution | Unrecognized solver message | The solver reported something ('`<kind>`') that this version of glmm / fastglmm does not recognize. Please report it at https://github.com/pawlenartowicz/glmm/issues. | Each port maps every note from the Rust solver to a warning kind. A note kind the port has no entry for is shown under this title, and the stored `kind` keeps the solver's own string. The solver is compiled into each package, so a released install should never show this; it means the package was built from mismatched sources. |
| `argument_ignored` | Note | Argument ignored | dispersion= has no effect for family '`<family>`'. | Each port checks these arguments before calling the solver, clears the ones that do not apply, and raises this note. Five cases: `dispersion=` on gaussian or negative binomial (gaussian estimates its residual variance, negative binomial its shape); quasi-likelihood `dispersion=` on a binomial or Poisson model with random effects; `init_theta=` / `init.theta=` on a family other than negative binomial; `warm_start` / `start` entries other than `beta` and `theta` (those two are still used); and `nagq` above 1 on a Gaussian model or a model without random effects, where nothing is approximated. Quasi-likelihood dispersion and `init_theta=` / `init.theta=` are not implemented yet for any model: passing them where they would apply raises an error instead of this note. |
| `argument_ignored` | Note | Argument ignored | Quasi-likelihood dispersion= is not supported yet for binomial or Poisson models. The default dispersion of 1 was used. | Same as above. |
| `argument_ignored` | Note | Argument ignored | init_theta= / init.theta= is not used for family '`<family>`'. | Same as above. |
| `argument_ignored` | Note | Argument ignored | warm_start accepts only 'beta' and 'theta'; these keys were ignored: `<keys>`. / start accepts only 'beta' and 'theta'; these elements were ignored: `<names>`. | Same as above. The keys are listed in the order they were given. |
| `argument_ignored` | Note | Argument ignored | nagq=`<k>` has no effect for `<a Gaussian model \| a model without random effects>`, because nothing is approximated. / nAGQ=`<k>` has no effect for … | Raised in place of `agq_fallback` when the model is Gaussian or has no random effects: neither has an integral to approximate, so nagq changes nothing. |
| `unused_grouping_levels` | Note | Unused grouping levels | Grouping factor '`<group>`' has levels with no rows (`<levels>`). They stay in the model with random effects of exactly zero and are counted in the number of groups. Remove unused categories before fitting. / Use droplevels() before fitting to remove them. | Raised when a plain (not nested or crossed) grouping factor declares levels that no row uses — pandas or pyarrow categories in Python, factor levels in R — and they sit between used levels. Each such level keeps a random-effect slot. An empty level adds only a constant to the likelihood, so the fixed effects, variance components and deviance do not change. It does appear as a zero row in the random effects, and it is counted in the number of groups the summary reports, which lme4 does not do. Unused levels after the last used one are left out silently and raise nothing. In R the usual cause is `na.action` or `subset=` removing every row of a level while the factor still declares it. |
| `re_design_scale_spread` | Note | Random-effect predictors on very different scales | The predictors with random slopes for '`<group>`' are on very different scales (ratio `<ratio>`). The fit is not affected, but the reported random-effect standard deviations are hard to compare. Rescaling these predictors makes them easier to read. | Checked once per grouping factor that has at least one random slope. glmm takes the root mean square of each random-slope column, and 1.0 for the intercept, and warns when the largest divided by the smallest is above 1000 — the same threshold as lme4's `checkScaleX`. The ratio is computed without row weights, because weights are not known at that point; the fit itself uses weighted values. Before fitting, glmm divides each random-slope column by its own root mean square, so the optimizer and its boundary tolerance see comparably scaled columns; the matching fixed-effect column keeps its units. The deviance, fixed effects and convergence therefore do not depend on this ratio. The reported random-effect standard deviations are converted back to each predictor's own units, which is why they can differ by orders of magnitude. |

Non-convergence warnings are chosen in this order: `no_coefficients`, then
`too_few_rows`, then `constant_response`, then by model: `design_unsolvable`
or `glm_diverged` without random effects, `search_limit` or `fit_failed` with
them. Exactly one is raised.

A kind the solver reports that this version does not know keeps the solver's
own `kind` string.
